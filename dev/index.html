DiscreteDynamicSurvivalAnalysis.jl

Fit SIR models to case line lists, in discrete time.

DiscreteDynamicSurvivalAnalysis.jl fits deterministic, mean-field SIR models, for one population or several interacting groups, to individual infection and removal times from a closed population. It gives posterior distributions for the transmission rates and $R_0$, the parameters of a geometric, negative binomial or other discrete phase-type infectious period, the initially infected fraction and, when it is unknown, the size of the population at risk. Records may be incomplete: a case can lack its infection or its removal time, or still be infectious when observation ends.

Discrete dynamic survival analysis (DDSA) treats the individual infection and removal times of a line list as survival data whose distributions come from a deterministic epidemic model. A discrete-time SIR map, run on the calendar grid of the data (e.g. days), gives:

  • the expected fraction $\tau$ of the population infected during the observation window;
  • the distribution $f$ of the infection times over the steps of the window.

The log-likelihood of the line list is then

\[\ell = \underbrace{\log \mathrm{Binomial}(K;\, N, \tau)}_{\text{final size}} + \underbrace{\log \mathrm{Multinomial}(\mathbf{n};\, K, f)}_{\text{infection times}} + \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i \log P(W = \delta_i)}_{\text{recovery intervals}},\]

with $K$ cases, infection-step counts $\mathbf{n}$, recovery intervals $\delta_i$, and an infectious period $W$ with a discrete phase-type (DPH) distribution: geometric, negative binomial (Erlang-like), or any DPH. Right-censored intervals and cases with only a removal time add their own terms. Integer parameters (the number of infectious stages $r$, an unknown population size $N$) are summed out exactly. The continuous parameters (transmission rate $\beta$ or $R_0$, the infectious-period parameter, the initially infected fraction $\rho$) are sampled with NUTS through Turing.jl.

DDSA is the discrete-time counterpart of the continuous-time dynamic survival analysis (DSA) of KhudaBukhsh et al., Di Lauro et al. and Vossler et al. This package implements the methods of the manuscript Discrete dynamic survival analysis of epidemic case line lists (in preparation), and its vignettes reproduce the manuscript's analyses.

What the package covers

  • Data: binning of raw event times into a LineList (linelist), with missing times, right-censoring and removal-only cases.
  • Forward maps: the GLCT (phase-type stages) and Boxcar (infection-age) SIR maps, for one or several groups with any force of infection (forward_map).
  • Likelihood: the DDSA likelihood and its terms (ddsa_loglikelihood).
  • Models: single-population models with geometric or negative binomial infectious periods (DDSAModel), and building blocks for custom Turing models (see the Hagelloch vignette, which also fits a Coxian infectious period on the GLCT map).
  • Inference: fit_ddsa samples the posterior, then draws $r$ and $N$ exactly. posterior_summary, posterior_predictive and recovery_predictive report the results; plot recipes become available when Plots.jl is loaded.
  • Simulation: synthetic line lists from the DDSA working model and from a stochastic chain binomial (simulate_linelist, simulate_chain_binomial).
  • Submodules: PhaseTypeDistributions (continuous and discrete phase-type distributions) and IntegerParameters (exact marginalisation of integer parameters). Their exports are re-exported.
  • Example data: the manuscript's data sets (example_data_path).

Installation

The package requires Julia ≥ 1.11. Install it from GitHub:

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/epirecipes/DiscreteDynamicSurvivalAnalysis.jl")

Quickstart

Simulate the synthetic outbreak of the manuscript's data-availability example: 99 susceptibles and one initial infective, with an Erlang-4 infectious period of mean 4 days discretised to daily steps, $\beta = 0.5$ per day, over 40 days.

using DiscreteDynamicSurvivalAnalysis, Distributions, Random

sim = simulate_linelist(Xoshiro(1234), dph_erlang_from_mean(4, 4.0, 1.0), 0.5, 0.01, 99, 40)
data = sim.data
LineList(nsteps = 40, Δt = 1.0, final_size = 91, known infection times = 91, recovery intervals = 92)

Fit a model with a negative binomial infectious period, with $r \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ stages summed out, and a known population of 99:

spec = DDSAModel(infectious_period = NegBinGLCT(1:10),
                 priors = (β = Uniform(1e-4, 2.5), ρ = Beta(1, 98), p = Beta(1, 1)),
                 population = 99)
fit = fit_ddsa(Xoshiro(1), spec, data; ndraws = 500, nchains = 2)
DDSAFit(NegBinGLCT, 2 chains × 500 draws, parameters: β, p, ρ, r)
